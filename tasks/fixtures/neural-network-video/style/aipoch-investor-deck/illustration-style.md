# Scientific editorial illustration

This is an investor-deck illustration language derived from selected visual
evidence in the external r5 lineage. The bundled images are reference-only,
not official assets and not reusable production artwork.

## Core language

- Black, precise line-drawn research people with diverse roles and poses.
- Scientific symbols such as DNA, molecules, books, microscopes, datasets,
  institutions, papers, charts, and computing systems.
- Regular halftone maps or structural dot fields that explain scale and
  topology.
- Canonical yellow connection arcs, nodes, directional paths, or one active
  stage.
- Isometric workflow islands when a process needs spatial separation.
- Large calm areas intentionally reserved for deck copy.
- Editorial geometry: registration marks, short rules, small crosses, and grid
  points used sparingly.

The illustration must explain a relationship: network, ecosystem, translation,
workflow, or feedback loop. It is not decorative wallpaper.

## Palette normalization

New artwork uses canonical deck colors:

- background: `#FCFCFA` or `#F7F7F4`;
- ink: `#111111`;
- secondary structure: `#6B6B66` and `#E7E5DE`;
- one connection focus: `#FBDD67`.

The external r5 evidence contains older off-whites, yellows, and grays. Treat
those only as source observations and translate them to the roles above. Do not
sample legacy colors directly into a new asset.

- `#F7F5EF` / `#E8E8E8` → `#FCFCFA` / `#F7F7F4`
- `#ECD44C` → `#FBDD67`
- `#626262` → `#6B6B66`

## Light illustration

1. Start with a warm-white field and black editorial line work.
2. Reserve 35–50% clean negative space for title and evidence.
3. Use a regular halftone structure only where it communicates geography,
   density, or system scale.
4. Use yellow for one connected path, active stage, or small set of nodes.
5. Keep shadows limited to a very soft, short object-grounding shadow within
   isometric scenes.
6. Ensure all meaningful symbols remain legible when the full slide is viewed
   as a thumbnail.

## Dark illustration

Dark artwork must be composed natively:

- `#111111` field;
- `#FCFCFA` principal line work;
- `#B4B4AE` people or secondary geometry;
- `#4E4E49` regular map/structure dots;
- one `#FBDD67` node, arc, or route.

Do not make dark artwork by inverting a light image. Redraw hierarchy,
occlusion, and negative space for black. Do not add glow around yellow nodes;
contrast comes from line weight, scale, and empty space.

## Composition families

### Global research network

A halftone globe or map carries a sparse set of researchers, institutions, and
scientific symbols. Yellow arcs show collaboration. Keep one side open for a
thesis and avoid duplicate world-map compositions within the same deck.

### Research ecosystem ring

Actors and symbols form a circular system around a deliberately empty center.
Use when showing a shared infrastructure or ecosystem, not a linear process.

### Three-stage translation

Three framed scenes connect left to right: discovery/complexity,
collaboration/coordination, and validation/impact. Keep stages visually
parallel and labels outside the illustration.

### Research workflow flywheel

Five or six isometric stations form a clockwise loop. Arrows and one active
yellow station establish motion. Use only for a genuinely recurring process.

## Prohibited treatment

- random paper grain, photocopy damage, scratches, or faux aging;
- yellow-green glow, gradient aura, neon, particles, or lens effects;
- oversized shadow behind the whole image;
- emoji, photorealistic stock scientists, or generic corporate 3D characters;
- dense micro-details that disappear in presentation view;
- embedded financing figures, product UI, team identities, or confidential
  source text;
- direct reuse of the bundled reference images in a published deck.

## Production checklist

- [ ] The image communicates one named relationship.
- [ ] Light or dark hierarchy is native to its canvas.
- [ ] Palette uses canonical roles and one yellow focus.
- [ ] Negative space matches the intended title placement.
- [ ] Halftone is regular and structural.
- [ ] No text is baked into the image unless explicitly required.
- [ ] No sensitive source content or recognizable product screenshot appears.
- [ ] The deck cites newly commissioned/generated artwork as appropriate.
